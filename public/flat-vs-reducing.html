Flat rate vs reducing balance loans

A flat-rate loan calculates interest using the original principal for the agreed period. A reducing-balance loan calculates interest against the outstanding principal as the loan is repaid.

How flat-rate interest works

Flat interest = Principal × Annual rate × Term in years

The principal and flat interest are then spread across the scheduled payments.

How reducing-balance interest works

With an amortising reducing-balance loan, each payment covers interest and part of the principal. As the outstanding principal falls, the interest portion generally falls too.

Why borrowers should compare the method

The same advertised rate can lead to different costs when the interest method is different. Before accepting a loan offer, ask whether the rate is flat or reducing and ask for the total amount you will repay.

Frequently asked questions

Which method usually costs less?

For the same principal, rate and term, a standard reducing-balance calculation usually produces less total interest than a simple flat-rate calculation.

Does the calculator include fees?

No. Processing fees, insurance, penalties and other lender charges are not included.

Can lenders calculate loans differently?

Yes. Actual contracts can use different schedules, fees, rates or interest conventions. Treat the results as estimates.
