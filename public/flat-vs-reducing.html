What is a flat-rate loan?

With a simple flat-rate calculation, interest is based on the original principal for the whole agreed term. The total interest can therefore be calculated as principal × annual rate × years.

Flat interest = Principal × Annual rate × Term in years

The principal plus this interest is then divided across the scheduled payments.

What is a reducing-balance loan?

A reducing-balance loan calculates interest on the outstanding principal. As you repay the loan, the balance falls and less interest is charged on the remaining balance.

Most standard amortising loan calculators use the reducing-balance formula for equal periodic payments.

Why the difference matters

Two lenders can advertise the same annual percentage but produce different total costs if one uses flat interest and the other uses a reducing balance. Always ask how the lender calculates interest before comparing offers.

Frequently asked questions

Is a flat rate the same as a reducing rate?

No. They calculate interest on different bases, so the same stated rate can have a different effective cost.

Which method is cheaper?

For the same principal, rate and term, a standard reducing-balance calculation will usually produce less interest than a simple flat-rate calculation.

Does this include lender fees?

No. Processing fees, insurance, penalties and other charges should be considered separately.
