Flat Rate vs Reducing Balance

Learn how the two common loan interest methods work, understand the difference in total cost, and compare them using the free FigureNG calculator.

What is a flat-rate loan?

With a flat-rate loan, interest is generally calculated using the original principal for the agreed loan period. The interest does not reduce simply because you have paid down part of the principal.

For example, suppose you borrow ₦1,000,000 at a flat rate of 20% per year for six months. The estimated interest would be ₦100,000 because 20% is applied to ₦1,000,000 for half a year.

Important: A flat-rate percentage can look relatively low because the calculation is based on the original loan amount. The actual cost should therefore be considered together with the repayment schedule and any fees.

What is reducing-balance interest?

With reducing-balance interest, interest is calculated using the outstanding principal balance.

As you make principal repayments, the outstanding balance generally falls. Future interest calculations therefore use a smaller balance.

For an amortizing loan, this usually results in less total interest than applying the same nominal rate as a flat rate over the same period.

Flat rate vs reducing balance comparison

Why the interest rate alone is not enough

Two lenders can advertise the same percentage but produce different repayment amounts if they use different interest methods or charge different fees.

When comparing loans, check the interest calculation method, loan duration, processing fees, insurance, management fees, late-payment charges and the total amount you will repay.

The amount you actually receive is also important if fees are deducted before the loan is disbursed.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, flat rate or reducing balance?

At the same stated interest rate and loan term, a typical reducing-balance calculation produces less interest than a flat-rate calculation because the interest base falls as principal is repaid.

Is a 20% flat rate the same as 20% reducing balance?

No. They use different interest bases. A 20% flat rate is generally calculated using the original principal, while a reducing-balance calculation uses the outstanding balance.

Why is my lender's result different from FigureNG?

Lenders may use different formulas, payment frequencies, rounding rules, fees, insurance charges or other contractual terms. FigureNG provides estimates and should not be treated as an official lender quotation.

Does a lower monthly payment always mean a cheaper loan?

No. A longer loan term can reduce the monthly payment while increasing the total interest or total repayment. Compare the total cost as well as the monthly payment.
