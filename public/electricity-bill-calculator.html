How the electricity bill calculator works

The basic calculation is simple:

Estimated electricity cost = Units consumed × Tariff per kWh

For example, if you use 100 kWh and your applicable tariff is ₦100 per kWh, the basic energy charge is approximately ₦10,000.

Your actual bill or vending cost can differ because other charges, taxes, adjustments, outstanding balances, credits, fees or the specific billing arrangement may apply.

Nigeria electricity service bands

NERC's Service-Based Tariff framework classifies customers into Bands A to E based on minimum expected hours of supply.

A 20+ hours
B 16+ hours
C 12+ hours
D 8+ hours
E 4+ hours

What is a unit of electricity?

On an electricity meter, a unit generally refers to one kilowatt-hour, written as kWh.

One kWh means using 1 kilowatt of electrical power for one hour. For example, a 100-watt appliance running continuously for 10 hours uses approximately 1 kWh.

Your meter measures electricity consumption in kWh. The number of units you receive when purchasing prepaid electricity therefore depends on the amount paid and the applicable tariff and charges.

How to find your electricity tariff

NERC states that your electricity tariff is indicated on your electricity bill issued by your DisCo or on your vending receipt if you use a prepaid meter.

That is why FigureNG asks you to enter your actual tariff rather than assuming that everyone in Nigeria pays the same price per kWh.

Tip: If you bought prepaid electricity recently, look at the vending receipt and check the units received and the amount paid. Your applicable tariff information may also be shown there.

Important information about Nigerian electricity tariffs

Nigeria does not have one universal electricity tariff that applies to every customer. The applicable rate can depend on the Distribution Company, customer category, service band and other regulatory factors.

NERC publishes tariff orders and reviews for electricity distribution companies. These rates can also change as regulatory orders are updated.

For this reason, FigureNG does not claim that a particular tariff is the current rate for every Nigerian electricity customer. Enter the tariff shown on your own bill or vending receipt for a more relevant estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does 100 units of electricity cost in Nigeria?

It depends on your applicable tariff. At ₦100 per kWh, 100 units would have a basic energy cost of ₦10,000 before any applicable additional charges or adjustments.

How much is one unit of electricity?

There is no single nationwide price that applies to every customer. Your applicable price per kWh depends on your electricity service and tariff. Check your bill or vending receipt.

What are Band A, B, C, D and E?

They are service bands under NERC's Service-Based Tariff framework. Band A has a minimum expected supply level of 20 hours, while Bands B, C, D and E have minimum levels of 16, 12, 8 and 4 hours respectively.

How do I calculate my electricity bill?

Multiply the electricity units consumed in kWh by the applicable tariff per kWh. Your actual bill may also contain other applicable charges, adjustments or taxes.

How do I know my electricity tariff?

NERC says customers can find their electricity tariff on their DisCo bill or prepaid vending receipt.

Can this calculator tell me exactly what my DisCo will charge?

No. It provides an estimate based on the units and tariff you enter. Your actual bill or vending transaction can include other charges and adjustments.
