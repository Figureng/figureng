What is VAT in Nigeria?

Value Added Tax, commonly called VAT, is a consumption tax applied to taxable supplies of goods and services under Nigeria's tax system.

For standard VAT calculations, the rate used by the Nigeria Revenue Service's current e-invoicing documentation is 7.5%. The NRS documentation identifies this as the standard VAT category.

Example: If a taxable amount is ₦100,000 and the applicable VAT rate is 7.5%, the VAT is ₦7,500 and the VAT-inclusive amount is ₦107,500.

How to calculate 7.5% VAT

Add VAT

₦100,000 → ₦107,500

VAT = ₦100,000 × 7.5% = ₦7,500.

Total including VAT = ₦107,500.

Remove VAT

₦107,500 → ₦100,000

When an amount already includes 7.5% VAT, the VAT portion is not calculated by simply subtracting 7.5% of the inclusive price.

The VAT-exclusive amount is calculated by dividing the inclusive amount by 1.075.

Important VAT information

Not every good or service necessarily receives the same VAT treatment. Nigerian tax rules provide for different treatments including standard-rated, zero-rated and exempt supplies.

The NRS's current e-invoicing documentation, for example, identifies standard VAT at 7.5% and also provides separate categories for zero-rated and other VAT treatments.

Therefore, this calculator should be used as a general calculation tool and not as a determination that a particular transaction is legally subject to VAT.

Frequently asked questions

What is the VAT rate in Nigeria in 2026?

The standard VAT rate used in the Nigeria Revenue Service's current e-invoicing documentation is 7.5%.

How much is 7.5% VAT on ₦100,000?

7.5% of ₦100,000 is ₦7,500. If VAT is added to ₦100,000, the total is ₦107,500.

How do I remove 7.5% VAT from a price?

If the price already includes 7.5% VAT, divide the VAT-inclusive amount by 1.075 to calculate the amount before VAT. The difference is the VAT component.

Is VAT always 7.5% in Nigeria?

7.5% is the standard VAT rate used in the calculator, but specific supplies can have different VAT treatments, including exemptions and zero-rated supplies.

Can I use this calculator for business invoices?

You can use it for general VAT calculations, but businesses should apply the applicable Nigerian tax rules to the specific transaction and verify their invoicing and compliance requirements with the Nigeria Revenue Service or a qualified tax professional.

Is FigureNG a tax authority?

No. FigureNG is an independent calculator and information website. It does not represent the Nigeria Revenue Service and does not provide official tax rulings or tax advice.
