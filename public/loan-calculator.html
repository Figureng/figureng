Why flat rate vs reducing balance matters

The same advertised interest rate can result in very different repayment costs depending on how interest is calculated.

With a reducing-balance loan, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal. As you repay the loan, the balance falls and the interest charged generally falls as well.

With a flat-rate loan, interest is calculated using the original principal throughout the stated term. The interest does not fall simply because you have already repaid part of the principal.

Research on loan products has found both flat and declining balance methods in use, which makes knowing the method important when comparing loan offers.

How the calculations work

Reducing balance

The monthly payment is calculated using an amortization formula. Each payment contains both interest and principal. As the outstanding balance falls, the interest component generally falls too.

Monthly Payment = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Flat rate

The flat-rate calculation applies the stated rate to the original principal for the applicable loan period and then spreads the resulting interest across the repayment period.

Total Interest = Loan Amount × Rate × Loan Period
Do not compare loan rates by percentage alone. Before accepting a loan, check whether the quoted rate is flat or reducing balance. Also check processing fees, insurance, documentation fees, late-payment charges and the total amount you will actually repay.

Frequently asked questions

What is a reducing-balance loan?

Interest is calculated on the outstanding loan balance. As principal is repaid, the balance falls and future interest charges generally decrease.

What is a flat-rate loan?

A flat rate calculates interest using the original loan amount for the stated period instead of reducing the interest base as principal is repaid.

Which is cheaper, flat rate or reducing balance?

For the same nominal rate and term, a flat-rate calculation will generally produce more interest than a reducing-balance calculation because interest continues to be based on the original principal.

Can I enter a monthly interest rate?

Yes. Select "Monthly rate" when the lender quotes the interest rate per month. The calculator will use that rate for the loan calculation.

Does the calculator include processing fees?

No. The calculator focuses on principal and interest. Always add lender fees, insurance and other compulsory charges when determining the real cost of borrowing.

Is this calculator an official lender quotation?

No. FigureNG provides estimates for informational purposes. The lender's signed offer and repayment schedule should always be treated as the authoritative figures for a loan.
