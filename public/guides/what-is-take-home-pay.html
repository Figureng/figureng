What Is Take-Home Pay?

Gross salary is not necessarily the amount that reaches your bank account. Here's what take-home pay means and why it can be different from your stated salary.

FigureNG · Updated August 2026

Take-home pay is the amount of money you actually receive after applicable taxes and deductions have been taken from your gross income.

Gross salary vs take-home pay

Gross salary is the amount stated before relevant deductions. Take-home pay is the amount remaining after applicable deductions and taxes have been accounted for.

This means two people earning the same gross salary can potentially receive different amounts depending on their individual deductions and circumstances.

What can reduce your take-home pay?

Depending on the employee and applicable rules, deductions can include taxes and certain qualifying contributions or other deductions.

The exact items that apply can vary, which is why simply subtracting one fixed percentage from gross salary may not give an accurate estimate.

Why monthly salary can be misleading

When people compare salaries, they often compare the gross monthly figure. But the amount available for spending is the take-home amount.

For example, an advertised salary of ₦300,000 per month does not automatically mean the employee receives ₦300,000 in their account.

Annual income matters too

Many tax calculations are based on annual figures. Converting a monthly salary to an annual amount can therefore make it easier to understand the overall calculation.

What should you look at when comparing jobs?

  • Gross annual salary.
  • Estimated monthly take-home pay.
  • Pension and other employee contributions.
  • Tax treatment.
  • Allowances and benefits.

The bottom line

Take-home pay gives you a more practical picture of how much income may actually be available after applicable deductions and tax.

When evaluating a job offer or planning your finances, it is useful to understand both the gross figure and the estimated net amount.
