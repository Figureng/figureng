How much loan can I afford in Nigeria?

Loan affordability is about finding a repayment amount that fits your income after considering your existing debts. A loan can look attractive because of its advertised amount, but the monthly repayment is what affects your regular cash flow.

How the FigureNG loan affordability calculator works

  1. Enter your monthly net income.
  2. Enter your current monthly debt payments.
  3. Choose a maximum debt-to-income ratio for your estimate.
  4. Enter the expected interest rate and loan term.
  5. Calculate the estimated loan amount that fits the remaining repayment budget.

What is a debt-to-income ratio?

Debt-to-income ratio compares monthly debt payments with monthly income. For example, a 35% ratio means total monthly debt payments are limited to about 35% of the income used in the calculation. Different lenders can use different limits, so this calculator lets you change the assumption.

Why existing loans matter

If you already repay a loan, salary advance, credit facility or another qualifying debt, that payment reduces the amount available for a new loan. Entering your existing monthly debt gives a more realistic affordability estimate.

Example

If monthly net income is ₦250,000 and the chosen debt-to-income ratio is 35%, the total monthly debt budget is ₦87,500. If existing debt payments are ₦20,000, about ₦67,500 remains for the new loan payment. The calculator then converts that payment into an estimated loan amount using the selected interest rate and term.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much loan can I afford based on my salary?

It depends on your income, existing debts, interest rate and repayment term. Use your net monthly income for this estimate.

Does this calculator guarantee loan approval?

No. It only estimates affordability. The lender makes the final decision.

Should I use gross or net salary?

For a household affordability estimate, net monthly income is usually more useful because it reflects the money available after deductions.

Can I change the debt-to-income ratio?

Yes. The default is 35%, but you can test another assumption.
