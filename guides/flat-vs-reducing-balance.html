Flat Rate vs Reducing Balance Loans

The interest rate alone does not tell the whole story. The method used to calculate that interest matters too.

FigureNG · Updated August 2026

When comparing loans, borrowers often focus on the advertised interest rate. But two loans with the same stated annual rate can produce different repayment costs when different interest calculation methods are used.

What is a flat rate?

With a flat-rate method, interest is generally calculated against the original principal for the agreed period. The fact that you are gradually repaying the principal does not necessarily reduce the interest base used in the simple flat-rate calculation.

What is a reducing balance?

With a reducing-balance method, interest is calculated against the outstanding principal. As repayments reduce the amount still owed, the interest portion can also reduce over time.

Why can the repayments differ?

Consider a loan where the original principal is ₦1,000,000. If interest is calculated as a flat percentage of the original principal, the calculation continues to use that original amount as its base.

Under a reducing-balance structure, the outstanding principal changes as payments are made. Consequently, the interest calculation changes during the repayment period.

Important: The exact repayment depends on the lender's calculation method, payment schedule, fees and other loan terms.

What should you compare?

  • The actual amount you receive.
  • The total interest payable.
  • Processing and other fees.
  • The total amount you will repay.
  • The repayment frequency and schedule.

Look beyond the advertised rate

A loan advertised at a particular annual rate should not be judged by that percentage alone. The calculation method and additional charges can materially affect the total cost.

Before accepting a loan, compare the amount you receive with the total amount you will eventually repay.
