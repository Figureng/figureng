How Nigerian PAYE Tax Works in 2026

A straightforward explanation of chargeable income, progressive tax bands and how PAYE affects estimated take-home pay.

FigureNG · Updated August 2026

PAYE means Pay As You Earn. It is the system through which employment income tax is generally collected from employees. Instead of waiting until the end of the year to settle the whole liability, tax is normally deducted through payroll.

What determines your PAYE tax?

The basic idea is to start with relevant income, account for eligible deductions and reliefs, determine the resulting chargeable income, and then apply the applicable progressive tax rates.

What is chargeable income?

Chargeable income is the amount on which the applicable income tax calculation is based after relevant deductions and reliefs have been taken into account.

This distinction is important because your gross income is not necessarily the same as the income used for the tax calculation.

Progressive tax rates

A progressive tax structure means different portions of chargeable income can be taxed at different rates. The higher rate applies to the portion that falls within the higher band, rather than automatically applying that rate to every naira earned.

Why this matters

A person moving into a higher tax band does not normally mean their entire income suddenly becomes taxable at that higher rate.

What about deductions?

Depending on the applicable rules and the individual's circumstances, certain deductions or reliefs may affect the calculation.

For this reason, two employees with the same gross income can potentially have different estimated tax outcomes if their qualifying circumstances are different.
